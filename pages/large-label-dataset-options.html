Dataset decision brief · 08 Oct 2026

Pick the pair that makes the paper credible.

The search surfaced one clean same-domain pair for a training-free, large-label intent pipeline: MASSIVE + HWU64. It preserves an applied dialogue-systems story without giving up the general-purpose argument.

Bottom line

Use two conversational-assistant datasets as the main evaluation. Keep legal as a domain-prestige fallback, and WOS-46985 as an optional high-label stress test.

MASSIVE

60 intents

What the dataset represents. MASSIVE is a multilingual natural-language-understanding collection built around single-turn requests to a general intelligent voice assistant. Each short utterance is assigned one intent inside one of 18 scenarios, so the model must distinguish closely related requests rather than broad topics.

Application idea. Use it to prototype the intent router behind a general voice assistant: an incoming request is mapped to one of 60 actions before the appropriate skill or workflow is invoked.

HWU64

64 intents

What the dataset represents. HWU64 collects very short home-domain utterances for human–robot interaction. Its 64 intents span 21 domains and include practical tasks, robot commands and chit-chat, creating a second assistant setting with a different collection distribution.

Application idea. Use it to prototype a home-assistant or domestic-robot command router that turns a brief spoken instruction into the correct capability while keeping the full intent catalogue outside Laya’s decision window.

01

MASSIVE + HWU64

Conversational assistant / NLU

Best overall. Both are short-text, single-label and fine-grained; the pair supports an applied dialogue-systems paper while testing transfer across general-assistant and home-robot settings.

MASSIVE asks for one intent from 60 based on a single assistant utterance. Its scenario structure gives the shortlist stage many nearby candidates, making recall@k a meaningful test rather than an easy topic match.

HWU64 asks the same kind of question over 64 intents, but with home-domain human–robot language across tasks, commands and chit-chat. That makes it a useful transfer set instead of a near-duplicate of MASSIVE.

Application sketchA two-stage intent router for voice assistants or domestic robots: embeddings retrieve the likely actions, then Jev or Laya chooses the final action from that reduced list.

02

LEDGAR + EUR-LEX

Legal · contracts + legislation

Best domain-prestige fallback for legal AI. It offers two recognizable legal document settings, but only LEDGAR matches the pipeline natively; EUR-LEX requires a clearly documented conversion because a law can have several labels.

LEDGAR contains short contract provisions from US SEC filings, each assigned one of 100 provision types. The text is usually only one to three sentences, so it tests fine-grained legal clause classification without making document length the main difficulty.

EUR-LEX contains full EU legislative documents tagged with 100 EuroVoc concepts. It is long-text and multi-label, so reducing it to a single choice changes the task and weakens a direct comparison with LEDGAR.

Application sketchLEDGAR could drive a contract-review triage tool that routes each clause to its provision category. EUR-LEX could support legislative indexing by suggesting relevant concepts, but the experiment must preserve or explicitly simplify its multi-label nature.

03

Reuters-21578 + RCV1

Newswire topic classification

A genuine same-domain news pair, but a weak practical choice for this paper. Both datasets assign multiple topics to a document, and RCV1 adds access and hosting friction, so the evaluation would spend substantial effort adapting the task.

Reuters-21578 is a collection of 1987 Reuters newswire articles with 90 ModApte topics. Its medium-length articles and overlapping topic labels make it a document-tagging benchmark rather than a clean one-intent choice task.

RCV1 is the larger 1996–97 Reuters corpus with 103 topics and roughly 804,000 documents. It supplies scale and a later news distribution, but it is also multi-label and requires an RCV1 access agreement through NIST.

Application sketchAn automated newsroom tagging system could shortlist likely desks or subject codes for each incoming article and then assign the final topic set. That is useful, but it no longer matches a single-choice Jev/Laya evaluation without task conversion.

S1

WOS-46985

Academic subject classification

The strongest standalone stress test. WOS-46985 contains 46,985 academic paper abstracts organized into 134 fine-grained subject classes under seven broader parents. Closely related research areas put real pressure on the embedding shortlist, although no clean 50+ label partner was found.

Unlike the assistant datasets, this is a short-to-medium document task rather than a spoken-request task. That makes it useful for checking whether the same retrieve-then-decide pipeline transfers beyond intent routing while keeping a single-label target.

Application sketchAn academic indexing or submission-routing tool could read an abstract, retrieve the most plausible subject categories, and let the decision model select the final fine-grained field for cataloguing or editorial triage.

S2

MTOP

Task-oriented dialogue NLU

A compelling third conversational dataset if its release terms work for the paper. MTOP covers about 100,000 task-oriented utterances in six languages, with 117 intent types across 11 domains; the English portion contains 22,288 utterances.

Its multilingual, task-oriented setup would test whether the shortlist survives both a larger label space and language variation. It remains a secondary option because the research did not verify a permissive release license.

How to frame the paper

The dataset pair strengthens generality. It does not remove the need for classifier baselines.

Don’t claim “not comparable.”

Claim a different operating point: training-free, label-flexible classification under latency and cost constraints. Then compare against neural classifiers as a supervised reference rather than pretending the comparison is invalid.

1 · Shortlist qualityReport recall@k across several k values. This is the hard ceiling on end-to-end accuracy.
2 · End-to-end qualityMeasure final intent accuracy and errors by confusion cluster on both datasets.
3 · Necessary baselinesInclude embedding top-1, direct all-label Jev/Laya where feasible, shortlisted decision model, and a supervised classifier reference.
4 · Systems evidenceReport latency and cost with the same hardware, batching and label descriptions across both datasets.
5 · CalibrationIf confidence is part of the value proposition, measure it rather than treating model scores as calibrated by default.
6 · Transfer claimUse the same pipeline and protocol unchanged on MASSIVE and HWU64; let distribution shift be the evidence.

Open questions before you lock the benchmark

These are research gaps, not reasons to abandon the recommendation.

HWU64 split protocol

The native distribution uses 10-fold cross-validation; a commonly used fold is 9,960 train / 1,076 test, but the research did not find one canonical fixed split. Pre-register the fold strategy.

MTOP license

The exact release terms were not verified. Treat it as unavailable for the main paper until the Meta release license is confirmed.

WOS split counts

The Hugging Face upload shows a train split, but exact train/test counts were not verified. Confirm before writing the methods section.

Domains that did not yield a pair

No second 50+ finance-intent dataset was found beyond Banking77; no suitable 50+ medical short-text single-label set was found; e-commerce options were either below 50 labels or extreme-scale multi-label.
